# Medication and treatments

## Giving medication on time
Prescription medication is always high priority. If the vet prescribed a dose at
set times (for example twice a day, 12 hours apart), give it a fixed start time
rather than letting it float. Pilling a pet or giving liquid medication takes
about 5 minutes; allow 10 if the pet resists.

## Twice-daily medication
Medication given twice a day is best scheduled as two separate fixed-time tasks,
for example a morning dose and an evening dose about 12 hours apart, each daily
and high priority.

## Flea, tick and worming treatments
Spot-on flea and tick treatments are usually monthly; tablets vary. Worming is
typically every three months for adults. Each takes about 5 minutes. Within a
weekly plan these are low-frequency tasks; mark them high priority on the day
they are due.

## Subcutaneous fluids and injections
Insulin for diabetic pets must be given at fixed times, usually twice daily with
meals, and is high priority. Subcutaneous fluids for kidney disease take 10 to
15 minutes and are high priority on the days the vet prescribes.
