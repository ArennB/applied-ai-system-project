# Senior pets

## When a pet counts as senior
Cats are generally considered senior from about 11 years old, and dogs from about
7 to 10 years depending on size (large breeds age sooner). Senior pets benefit
from a predictable routine and closer monitoring.

## Senior exercise
Senior dogs still need daily walks, but shorter and gentler: two walks of 15 to
20 minutes rather than one long one. Senior cats need gentle play of about 10
minutes a day to keep joints moving. Keep exercise daily and medium to high
priority; stop if the pet shows pain or stiffness.

## Senior cat kidney disease
Chronic kidney disease is common in older cats. Care often includes a
prescription renal diet, extra water (fountains or wet food), and medication or
subcutaneous fluids on a schedule set by the vet. Giving medication or fluids is
high priority and must happen at the time the vet specified, usually as a fixed
daily task taking 5 to 15 minutes.

## Senior health checks
Senior pets should see the vet every six months. At home, a weekly 5 minute
check of weight, appetite, water intake, lumps and mobility helps catch problems
early; it is a weekly, medium-priority task.
