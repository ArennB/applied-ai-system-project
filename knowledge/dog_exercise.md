# Dog exercise

## High-energy breeds
Working and sporting breeds such as huskies, border collies, Australian
shepherds, labradors, retrievers, pointers and malinois need a lot of daily
exercise: roughly 60 to 120 minutes a day for a healthy adult, split into at
least two sessions. A morning walk or run of 30 to 45 minutes is a sensible
anchor. Treat at least one daily walk as high priority, because an
under-exercised high-energy dog tends to become destructive or anxious.

## Moderate-energy breeds
Many mixed breeds, beagles, spaniels, poodles and terriers do well on 30 to 60
minutes a day, usually two walks of 15 to 30 minutes. One daily walk is high
priority; a second walk or a play session is medium priority.

## Low-energy and small breeds
Toy breeds, bulldogs, pugs, basset hounds, shih tzus and greyhounds at rest need
around 20 to 40 minutes a day. Brachycephalic (flat-faced) breeds such as pugs
and bulldogs overheat easily, so keep walks to 15 to 20 minutes in warm weather
and avoid the hottest part of the day.

## Splitting exercise into tasks
A daily walk is typically a daily, high-priority task of 20 to 45 minutes. Fetch,
tug or a training game works well as a separate 10 to 15 minute medium-priority
task. If time is tight, keep one walk and shorten it rather than dropping
exercise entirely.
